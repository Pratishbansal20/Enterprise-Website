**Short answer: choose the hinge by how the shutter sits on the cabinet (0 crank for full overlay, 8–9 crank for half overlay, inset for shutters inside the frame), choose soft-close if you want quiet shutters, and choose drawer channels a little shorter than the inside depth of the cabinet.** Most kitchen shutters need two hinges; tall ones need more.

## What are the types of kitchen hinges?

Modular kitchens use **concealed hinges** (also called auto hinges): a cup fits into a round hole drilled in the shutter, and an arm clips onto a mounting plate on the cabinet side. The main choice is the **crank**, which decides where the shutter sits.

| Hinge type | Also called | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full overlay** | 0 crank, straight arm | The shutter covers the whole side panel (end cabinets, single units) |
| **Half overlay** | 8 or 9 crank | Two shutters share one middle panel |
| **Inset** | 16–18 crank | The shutter sits inside the cabinet frame, flush with the front |

## Soft-close or normal hinges?

**Soft-close hinges** have a built-in damper that slows the shutter for the last few centimetres, so it closes quietly without slamming. They cost more but protect shutters and are now the norm in new kitchens. **Normal auto hinges** snap shut and suit budget work and utility cabinets.

## How many hinges does a shutter need?

A common carpenter’s rule of thumb, adjusted for heavy or wide shutters:

| Shutter height | Hinges |
| --- | --- |
| Up to about 900 mm (3 ft) | 2 |
| About 900–1,600 mm | 3 |
| About 1,600–2,000 mm | 4 |

Heavy shutters (thick boards, glass, acrylic) may need one extra hinge.

## What about corner and special cabinets?

- **Wide-angle hinges** (around 165–170°) let shutters open fully so pull-out baskets can come out.
- **Corner hinges** handle blind-corner and L-shaped cabinets.
- **Glass-door hinges** and **aluminium profile shutters** need their own fittings.

## Which drawer channel should I use?

| Channel type | What it does | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Telescopic (ball-bearing), full extension** | Drawer opens all the way out | Most kitchen and wardrobe drawers |
| **Soft-close telescopic** | Closes gently on its own | Kitchens, bedside drawers |
| **Heavy-duty (e.g. 45 kg rated)** | Carries heavier loads | Pots, pans, tall pull-outs |

**Length:** pick the channel closest to, but not longer than, the inside depth of the cabinet. A standard base unit about 560–580 mm deep inside usually takes an 18" (450 mm) or 20" (500 mm) channel. We stock channels from 10" to 24".

## How do I fit lift-up wall cabinets?

Overhead shutters that open upwards use **gas lifts** (gas pumps). They come in different strengths; the right one depends on the shutter’s weight and size. Too weak and the shutter drifts down; too strong and it slams up. Tell us the shutter size and board thickness and we will suggest the rating.

## Does the steel grade matter?

Yes. **SS304 stainless steel** hinges and baskets resist rust much better than lower grades in a wet, hot kitchen. They cost more but last longer near the sink and stove.

## Checklist before you buy

1. Number of shutters, with height and width of each.
2. Which shutters are full overlay, half overlay or inset.
3. Number of drawers and the inside depth of the cabinets.
4. Any lift-up, corner or tall units.
5. Soft-close or normal.
