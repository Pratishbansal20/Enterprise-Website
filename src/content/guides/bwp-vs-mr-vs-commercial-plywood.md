**Short answer: use BWP plywood wherever it can get wet (kitchen, bathroom, near a sink or outside wall), MR plywood for furniture in dry rooms, and commercial plywood only for low-cost or temporary work.** The difference comes down to the glue (resin) that holds the layers together and how well it stands up to water.

## What do BWP, MR and commercial mean?

| Grade | What it stands for | Resin (glue) | Handles | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **BWP** | Boiling Water Proof | Phenol formaldehyde (PF) | Direct water, steam, splashes | Kitchens, bathroom vanities, sink units, doors facing outside |
| **MR** | Moisture Resistant | Urea formaldehyde (UF) | Humidity, but not standing water | Wardrobes, beds, TV units, study tables in bedrooms and halls |
| **Commercial** | Trade name for basic interior ply | Usually UF | Dry conditions only | Temporary work, packing, low-budget furniture |
| **Marine** | Made to IS 710 | PF, better-quality veneers | Constant wet conditions | Boats, very wet areas, premium kitchens |

BWP costs more than MR because of the phenolic resin and stricter testing. Marine plywood costs more again.

## Which plywood should I use in each room?

- **Kitchen (base and sink units):** BWP. Water spills, steam and wet mopping reach the bottom of base cabinets every day.
- **Kitchen wall units:** BWP is safest; MR works if the units are well away from the sink and hob.
- **Bathroom vanity:** BWP, or consider [PVC or WPC boards](/products/pvc-wpc-board-hazaribag/) which don’t absorb water at all.
- **Wardrobes, beds, TV units:** MR is fine in a dry room. Choose BWP for a wardrobe on a wall that gets damp in the monsoon.
- **Main door frame and shutters on an outside wall:** BWP, or a ready [flush door](/products/plywood-shop-hazaribag/).

## How do I check the grade when I buy?

1. **Look for the stamp.** Every genuine sheet has the brand, grade (BWP or MR) and the Indian Standard it is made to printed on the face. No stamp is a warning sign.
2. **Buy from an authorised dealer and keep the bill.** Manufacturers usually ask for the bill before honouring a warranty.
3. **Don’t rely on the word “waterproof” alone.** Ask which grade it is and check the stamp yourself.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using MR plywood under the kitchen sink** to save money. It is usually the first part of a kitchen to swell and fail.
- **Leaving edges open.** Even BWP lasts longer when the edges are covered with laminate or edge banding, because water enters a board through its edges.
- **Mixing grades without telling the carpenter.** Mark BWP sheets clearly at site so they go into the kitchen and not into a bedroom.

## What thickness should I buy?

For cabinet bodies and shutters, 18 mm or 19 mm is standard. Use 12 mm for light shelves and drawer sides, and 6 mm or 9 mm for backs and drawer bottoms. Our [plywood page](/products/plywood-shop-hazaribag/) lists every thickness and sheet size we stock.
